Alright, let’s cut through the noise: the **Asus B550M-A AC** isn’t exactly a gold-standard audio board, but we can squeeze better low-latency performance out of it if you're doing music production, DJing, or just hate crackles in your ears.

### 🎧 Here's what to do:

#### 1. **Ditch the onboard audio for pro work**
The Realtek ALC887 chip is *fine* for gaming or Zoom calls. But for music? Meh. Use an external USB audio interface (Focusrite Scarlett, Motu M2, etc.) or a PCIe sound card if you’re tight on USB ports. This alone will massively improve latency and audio quality.

#### 2. **Update your BIOS & Chipset drivers**
- Get the latest BIOS from [ASUS Support](https://www.asus.com/motherboards-components/motherboards/prime/prime-b550m-a-ac/helpdesk_bios/)
- Update the AMD chipset drivers from AMD’s website, not ASUS:  
  👉 [https://www.amd.com/en/support](https://www.amd.com/en/support)

#### 3. **Power settings = Crucial**
- Control Panel > Power Options > Set to **High Performance**
- Then go into advanced settings and disable USB selective suspend
- Also disable CPU core parking using a tool like *ParkControl* (by Bitsum)

#### 4. **BIOS tuning for audio**
Go into your BIOS and:
- **Disable C-States**
- **Disable Spread Spectrum**
- **Enable HPET** if available
- Set PCIe to **GEN3** for stability (not AUTO)

#### 5. **LatencyMon or bust**
Download [LatencyMon](https://www.resplendence.com/latencymon) and run it while your DAW or audio software is working.
- Look for drivers causing DPC spikes: often it’s `ndis.sys` (network), `nvlddmkm.sys` (NVIDIA), or USB-related.
- Kill Wi-Fi/Bluetooth if not needed while producing.

#### 6. **Disable unused onboard devices**
In BIOS or Device Manager:
- Serial ports
- Onboard audio (if using external interface)
- Wi-Fi/Bluetooth (they suck for DPC)

### Here’s What’s Really Going On
Realtek chips are not designed for pro audio. They’re for system beeps, Zoom calls, and playing YouTube videos at 30ms latency. The **drivers suck**, the **buffer management is garbage**, and they often **hog CPU** when pushed below 256 samples. So when you switch from your external soundcard to onboard Realtek, you're basically switching from a racetrack to a gravel road.

---

### What You Can Do:

#### 🔧 1. **Install ASIO4ALL (but with caution)**
- It wraps the Realtek driver into something DAWs can better work with.
- Go to: [asio4all.org](http://asio4all.org), install it.
- In Ableton, choose **ASIO > ASIO4ALL v2** as your audio device.
- Open ASIO4ALL panel and **disable all other audio devices except Realtek**.

👉 *Caveat:* It’s still duct tape. Better than Windows Audio, but don’t expect miracles under 128 samples.

---

#### 🔧 2. **Reduce Plugin Load When Using Realtek**
Realtek can't handle low-latency plus heavy VSTs (especially synths, reverbs, or oversampling plugins). Try:
- Freezing tracks
- Flattening rendered instruments
- Avoiding oversampled plugins like OTT, FabFilter Pro-L2, etc.

---

#### 🔧 3. **Set Up a “Non-Studio” Profile**
Create a separate **Ableton Template** when you’re on Realtek:
- 512 samples buffer
- Only essential VSTs
- More pre-rendered clips
- Fewer sends and returns
Then **save this template** and only use it when you’re not on your main monitors.

---

#### 🧠 4. **Mental Shift: Don't Chase Low Latency on Realtek**
Unless you’re tracking live instruments or MIDI performances, latency isn’t your enemy—**crackles and dropouts are.** Keep it at 512 or even 1024 samples when you’re in this setup. Bounce later with the proper interface.

---

### If You Want a Real Fix
**Route the system audio to your external interface.** If your second system has an AUX/line out, plug it into an input on your interface and monitor everything through that. That way:
- You always use the low-latency drivers
- You never have to switch audio devices
- You keep Realtek out of the picture entirely

---

### One Last Wild Tip:
Try a USB-C **tiny DAC like the FiiO KA1** or **Audioquest Dragonfly Black**. Plug it in when you don’t want to use your interface. These small units give better latency and sound than Realtek—and work natively without ASIO weirdness.
